**Flex slots from here.** Roy Choi ran 2× Trafalgar Law (ST10-010) and 1× Sanji (OP07-064) over the third Satori and a Holly. **Caveat worth knowing:** Limitless's most recent tracked Enel results are from May 2026 (OP-15 format). The OP-16 core hasn't moved much per the deck guides, but if you want live OP-16 data, check [Limitless's Enel page](https://onepiece.limitlesstcg.com/decks/98) yourself before an event.

### Two things about how you play it

- **Fire the leader ability early, every game.** Don't hold it looking for a perfect turn. Your DON!! deck is 6 cards total, so every turn you don't use it is a turn of ramp you never get back — and the whole point is getting an Enel body above the format's removal ceiling before your opponent has developed.
- **Senor Pink is reactive, not proactive.** Play him the turn *after* you've spent an Event you want back. Playing him on an empty trash is a wasted card.

## 2. The meta after ST-31–36

**Your read, which I'm treating as the current picture:** Red/Green Luffy jumps to the top of the format, ahead of Purple Enel, and Green Mihawk is the other big winner off the new starters.

That's consistent with what's actually printed in the decks. Only 30 cards in the whole wave are new — 5 per starter — and they're heavily concentrated in exactly those two archetypes:

**Why Red/Green Luffy gained (ST-31, all {Straw Hat Crew} payoffs)**

| Card | Line |
|---|---|
| **ST31-001 Sanji** (5c, 3000, 1000 ctr) | [On Play] Draw 1 **and** play a ≤5-cost Straw Hat from hand. [DON!! ×2] gains [Rush]. A card-neutral two-body turn that can attack immediately. |
| **ST31-005 Thousand Sunny** (1c Stage) | [On Play] dig 5 for any Straw Hat. [Activate: Main] rest it to hand a rested DON!! to a Luffy. A 1-cost consistency engine — this is the quiet one that fixes the deck's draws. |
| **ST31-004 Monkey.D.Luffy** (7c, 9000) | [Rush] if you have 3+ given DON!!. [On Play] −1000 to an opposing Character per Straw Hat on your field. |
| **ST31-002 Jinbe** (5c, 6000) | [Blocker], [On Play] draw 1 + free 1-drop. |
| **ST31-003 Brook** (2c, 3000) | With 3+ given DON!!, a 6000 [Blocker] for 2 on the crack-back. |

Sanji + Sunny together are the buff: the deck now finds its pieces reliably *and* deploys two bodies a turn. That's a real jump, not a marginal one.

**Why Green Mihawk gained (ST-32, all {Slash} payoffs)**

| Card | Line |
|---|---|
| **ST32-003 Dracule Mihawk** (6c, 7000) | [On Play] if your Leader has {Slash}, **play a ≤5-cost [Perona] or {Slash} Character from hand for free**. Plus: when it rests on your turn, draw 1 discard 1. This is the card. |
| **ST32-004 Silvers Rayleigh** (4c, 5000) | Gains **[Rush: Character]** under a {Slash} Leader; [On Play] rests two ≤2-cost Characters. |
| **ST32-005 Roronoa Zoro** (1c, 2000, **2000 ctr**) | [Rush: Character] on a 1-drop that's also a 2000-counter. Absurd rate. |
| **ST32-001 Kin'emon** (1c, 2000) | Rest your {Slash} Leader or a DON!!: draw 2, discard 1. |
| **ST32-002 Kouzuki Oden** (5c, 6000) | Draw 1 and lock a ≤6-cost Character from being rested through your opponent's next end phase. |

Note the new keyword: **[Rush: Character]** — attacks Characters only, not the Leader, on the turn played. Green now has cheap immediate board interaction it never had.

**What the pre-starter trackers said**, for context on where things stood going in:

| Source (sampled pre-31 Jul) | Top of format |
|---|---|
| Limitless, late July | B/Y Nami ~25.8% · Purple Enel ~22.5% · G/B Luffy ~21.3% |
| opmetagame, 906 OP-16 decks | S: Purple Enel (14.9%, 65% WR), G/B Luffy (14.2%) · A: B/Y Teach, B/Y Nami · B: P/Y Rosinante, R/G Luffy, R/B Lucy, Black Yamato, Green Mihawk |

Red/Green Luffy and Green Mihawk were both sitting in B tier at ~4–5% share. If they're now the top two, that's a two-tier jump off five cards each — worth watching whether it holds through the first weekend of regionals or settles back.

**What it means for your Enel deck.** You're now the deck being hunted rather than the one hunting. Two adjustments to think about:

- **Red/Green Luffy is a race you can lose.** They deploy multiple bodies per turn and Sanji gives them rush. Your single-target removal (Gamma Knife) doesn't keep up with two-a-turn. This pushes the Gamma Knife count toward 3, and makes Senor Pink recursion more valuable, not less — recurring removal is how you fight a deck that rebuilds every turn.
- **Green Mihawk's ST32-003 is a 6-cost that brings a friend.** That's a lot of board out of one card. Same conclusion: you want more removal density and more counter events than the old stock list ran.

I'd hold off rebuilding around this until you've seen a weekend of results, but if you're testing this week, try the upgraded list with Gamma Knife at 3 (cut the Divine Departure) and see how the Luffy race feels in the sim.

**Your matchups as Enel:**

- **Red/Green Luffy** — the new one to prepare for. Two bodies a turn plus rush means you can't remove your way out. Prioritise Gamma Knife on whatever enables their next multi-body turn (Sanji, Jinbe) rather than the biggest body, and keep counter events for the turn your Enel needs to survive.
- **Green Mihawk** — ST32-003 turns one card into two bodies, one of which can be a rush threat. Same principle: kill the enabler, not the payoff.
- **Black/Yellow Teach** — was the hardest matchup pre-starters. Similar clock, better resource engine. Save 2–3 counter Events for the turn your finisher needs to survive; don't burn them protecting the Leader early.
- **Green/Blue Luffy** — goes wide, your removal is single-target. Pick the highest-value target each turn and let the oversized Enel body outpace the board rather than trying to answer everything.
- **Blue/Yellow Nami** — draw engine, grindier. You want to be as fast as possible.
- **Mirror** — whoever commits to the finisher first with counters in hand wins. Don't try to play the slow game.

## 3. What's fun (and cheap) right now

- **Blue Buggy (Impel Down swarm)** — C-tier competitively, genuinely fun to pilot. Wide boards, chaotic, cheap.
- **Black Yamato (Wano recursion)** — B-tier, ~4.3% share, plays like a proper grindy value deck. Rewarding without being a net-deck.
- **Purple Sengoku / Three Admirals** — the other Purple DON!! deck. Slower, more control-flavoured than Enel, and it uses a completely different set of cards, so it's a real change of pace.
- **Red/Blue Lucy** — 5.1% share, aggressive and straightforward. Good "second deck" if you want something that isn't a combo puzzle.
- **Red Shanks / Red Ace** — the classic beatdown decks. Cheap, fast, and you'll learn attack math properly.

**The new starters (ST-31 to ST-36)** — Red Luffy, Green Zoro, Blue Kuzan, Purple Katakuri, Red/Black Sabo, Yellow Kid. Each is a 51-card deck + 10 DON!! + one OP-16 booster; ST-35 Sabo is the only two-colour one. Five new cards each, all Block 5, all Standard legal.

Beyond the two that reshaped the meta, the ones worth knowing:

- **ST-33 Blue Kuzan** — a full discard-payoff Navy package. **ST33-004 Borsalino** is a 6-cost that costs −3 in a turn where you've trashed from hand, i.e. a 3-cost 6000 blocker with 2000 counter. **ST33-005 Garp** plays a free ≤8000 Navy body. Genuinely strong shell for the price.
- **ST-34 Purple Katakuri** — Big Mom DON!! ramp. **ST34-001 Katakuri** ramps 2 rested DON!! whenever you return a DON!! to the deck; **ST34-004 Linlin** is a 10-cost 12000 that zeroes a Character's base power and adds a Life card. Slow, but the payoff is real.
- **ST-35 Sabo** — Revolutionary Army recursion. **Koala (ST35-004)** and **Kuma (ST35-005)** both give your Leader a rested DON!! and play a ≤4000 Revolutionary Army body **from hand *or trash***. That's a lot of grind out of a starter.
- **ST-36 Kid** — Life manipulation. **ST36-002 Killer** has a [Trigger] that plays itself off a life card when your opponent is at ≤3 life. **ST36-004 Bartolomeo** is a 1-cost draw-2.

There's also a **Beginners Deck Party** format running 31 Jul – 30 Aug at participating stores: everyone plays one of the six starters unmodified. That's the cheapest possible way to drag friends into the game.

All 30 new cards are already loaded in the Deck Lab file — filter by set ST31 through ST36.

Also worth planning around: **OP-17 "World's Strongest Warriors"** lands 22 Aug (JP) / 28 Aug (EN). Every OP-16 card stays legal, so nothing you build now is wasted.

## 4. Simulators

| Tool | What it is | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **OPTCGSim** | Free desktop simulator, the community standard for online play | Full rules, real opponents, deck importing. This is what most people mean by "the sim." |
| **DuelVoyager** | Browser-based One Piece simulator + meta decklists | No install, ranked matches and replays |
| **Tabletop Simulator + OP mod** | Steam workshop mod | Requires buying TTS; you manually enforce rules, but total freedom |
| **Limitless Deck Builder (beta)** | `my.limitlesstcg.com/builder?game=OP` | Best for exporting straight into the sim |
| **Deck Lab** (the file I built you) | Deck builder + solitaire goldfish + exact draw odds | No opponent — it's for testing whether a list *flows* before you take it to a real sim |

The workflow I'd suggest: build and tune in Deck Lab, check the odds tab, then "Copy text list" and paste it into OPTCGSim to actually play games against people.
