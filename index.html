          How this works

          Card data is pulled live from the open-source punk-records dataset (scraped from Bandai's official card list) via jsDelivr CDN. Every English set through OP-16 is included, and new sets appear automatically as the dataset updates — no action needed from you.

          On ST-31 to ST-36 (English release 31 Jul 2026): the upstream dataset hasn't picked these up yet, so all 30 new cards are hard-coded into this file from the official English text — search ST31, ST32 … in the set filter and they're there. The reprints in those decks were already in the pool under their original card numbers. As soon as the dataset catches up, the live data takes over automatically and the hard-coded copies are ignored.

          One tile per card number. The raw data lists every printing separately — EB01-003, EB01-003_p1, EB01-003_p4 and so on. Those are the same card for deckbuilding, and the rarer printings are the ones whose art often isn't hosted anywhere, which is where blank tiles came from. The pool now shows the base printing only. If a card's art still can't be found on any host, you get a readable name/cost/power tile instead of a blank.

          Deck rules enforced: exactly 50 cards in the main deck, max 4 copies of any one card number, every card must share a colour with your Leader. The DON!! deck size is read from your Leader's text (Enel OP15-058 sets it to 6; everyone else is 10).

          Play vs AI is a real two-sided game. The engine runs the turn structure, DON!! economy, attacking, blocking, the counter step, life cards and triggers. Click one of your characters (or your Leader) to select it, then click a target — you can only attack the enemy Leader or a rested enemy character. Right-click a character to give it a DON!!. When the AI attacks you get a defence prompt offering your blockers, your counter cards and any [Counter] events you can pay for.

          Card effects are read off the printed text. Common patterns — draw, K.O., power modification, resting, DON!! ramp — resolve automatically. Anything the parser doesn't recognise pauses the game, shows you the text, and gives you shortcut buttons to apply it yourself. That trade-off is deliberate: it means every deck is playable, including your own brews, rather than only a curated card pool. Treat it as a practice tool for lines and race maths, not a rules authority.

          Not implemented: Double Attack, Banish, Stage abilities, and "when this card is trashed" effects. Those fall through to the manual prompt.

          The Advisor checks your deck two ways. Structural heuristics (counter density, curve, blockers, triggers, removal count) apply to any deck. Then it diffs your list against a real top-placing list for your Leader, showing what's missing, what's yours alone, and where copy counts differ.

          Goldfish Sim is the older solitaire playtest, kept because it's faster for testing opening hands: shuffle, mulligan, draw, gain DON!! on the correct schedule, play cards for their real cost, rest/attach DON!! to characters, and watch power update. There's no opponent — it's for testing whether a list flows: curve, DON!! economy, whether your finisher lands on turn 2. Leaders with a DON!! ramp ability (like Enel) get a dedicated button that follows the card text.

          Odds uses exact hypergeometric maths, not a simulation, so the numbers are precise. "By turn X" counts your opening 5 plus one draw per turn.

          Sharing: the share link encodes the whole deck in the URL fragment. Nothing touches a server. Give a friend this file once and you can then trade decks as links forever.
